How the Product Works

The biological reasoning for a NAD booster begins with the function of NAD+ in cellular metabolism. NAD+ can cycle between oxidised and reduced states, and helps in electron transfer in metabolic reactions. This is significant to pathways that produce energy from carbohydrates, fats, and other nutrients. NAD+ is also required by other enzymes involved in cellular signalling and DNA-related processes.

NAD+ metabolism may be affected with age and the impact of increased NAD+ availability on aspects of cellular function has been studied by researchers . Supplement ingredients that serve as NAD+ precursors are able to join the metabolic pathways that help to synthesise NAD+. However, increasing NAD+ or a related metabolite in blood or tissue is not necessarily the same as achieving a meaningful improvement in health, physical performance, longevity or disease outcomes.

Factors such as component ingredients, amounts, absorption, metabolism, and the nutritional and physiological status of the individual affect the effectiveness of any given formulation. Therefore, findings from research on individual ingredients or other NAD+-support formulas may not be applicable to Age Shield NAD Booster.

Potential benefits

One of the potential advantages of NAD+ support is the support of normal cellular energy metabolism. NAD+ is involved in cellular energy-producing processes, so is biologically important to maintain adequate availability. NAD+-precursor supplementation has been shown to influence NAD+-related biomarkers in some studies, but evidence for a broader therapeutic effect is less established.

NAD+ is also a biological cofactor for enzymes involved in cellular maintenance and biological stress responses. This has led to growing interest in supplementation with NAD+ in the light of healthy ageing and cellular resilience. But laboratory findings and mechanistic research should not be regarded as confirmation that a NAD booster reverses ageing or prevents age-related disease.

Potential Limitations & Risks

One important limitation of NAD+ supplements is that evidence for extended clinical results is still emerging. A supplement can influence a biochemical pathway without causing any measurable differences in symptoms or measurable health outcomes. Research on NAD+ precursors has also examined different compounds, doses, study lengths and populations, making it difficult to apply study results across all products.

There may also be potential reactions or interactions with supplement use. Who This Product May Suit

NAD-focused supplements could be of interest to adults with an interest in dietary strategies to cellular metabolism and healthy ageing. This category may be particularly interesting to those who want to examine how NAD+ biology relates to normal energy metabolism. But interest doesn’t necessarily equate to the need for supplementation, particularly for people who already have sufficient dietary intake.

Pregnant or nursing individuals, children, those with serious medical conditions and those who must take prescription drugs should be especially careful with dietary supplements. A health professional can determine whether the ingredients are appropriate given medications, medical history and nutritional needs.

People should also not rely on a NAD booster as an alternative to evidence-based health strategies. There is substantially more evidence in support of long-term health benefits of adequate sleep, physical activity, balanced nutrition, avoidance of tobacco, moderation of alcohol, and appropriate preventive healthcare than there is for any individual NAD-focused supplement.
